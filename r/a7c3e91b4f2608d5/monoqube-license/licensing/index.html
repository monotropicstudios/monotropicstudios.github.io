License Models and Rates

All rates are PROPOSALS for negotiation. Benchmarks are CITED. Nothing here is a legal opinion.

What the comparables say: hardware IP royalties cluster at 3% to 5% of the device price for architecture-level IP and about 10% when the licensor supplies a working subsystem. Per-unit flat fees (Avanci) work when the device price varies widely. MonoQube supplies a working reference design, firmware, protocol, and conformance program, which sits between Arm architecture and Arm CSS.

2. Model options

Model Structure Proposed rate Best for
A. Per-unit royalty (OEM) % of net selling price of each licensed product (module, PDU, rack, pod) 6% (units 1 to 100), 5% (101 to 1,000), 4% (above 1,000); floor $40 per unit; royalty base for a rack or pod is the metering and power-control subassembly, capped at $1,500 per rack (keeps it fair on a $3M rack) PDU, busway, rack, pod, and server OEMs
B. Per-device-hour (operator) Fee per authorized device-hour recorded in the ledger $0.002 per GPU-hour (0.02% of a B200 rental hour at $8.50) Neoclouds, colos, lessors running retrofit modules
C. Recurring service share Share of the licensee's MonoQube-based subscription revenue (witness, vault, reports) 20% OEMs that resell the service
D. Field-of-use Exclusive or non-exclusive rights limited to a field, for example "GPU-collateral finance in North America" or "defense tactical edge" Upfront fee + royalty A or B; exclusivity premium below Strategic licensees
E. Exclusive (field or territory) Exclusive in a defined field, with minimum annual royalties and diligence milestones Upfront $500k to $2M (PROPOSED, depends on field) + minimums starting at $250k/yr, stepping up One anchor partner per field
F. Non-exclusive standard Click-through style standard terms, same rate for all Model A or B, no upfront above $25k The ripple: many OEMs, fast
G. Acquisition A purchase of the application family is not offered in this data room. Any terms would be set with counsel. Not offered here Strategic acquirer

3. Recommended structure

  1. Non-exclusive standard license (F) with Model A for OEMs and Model B for operators. Same price for everyone in a tier so the first signers are not undercut. Most-favored-licensee clause for the first 10 licensees.
  2. One exclusive field-of-use (E) reserved for the first lender or lessor partner who mandates MonoQube in a facility, limited to "collateral attestation services for GPU-backed credit" for 24 months. This is a distribution deal, not a technology lockout. OEMs still sell hardware to everyone.
  3. Pilot conversion credit: pilot fees credit 100% against the first year of royalties.

3. What the claims do not cover (be candid in diligence)

  1. A signed or third-party-verifiable record is not in any claim. "Verifiable" is in the title only. Spec paragraphs [0039] and [0077] describe a cryptographically verifiable summary sent to an external system, so a continuation or amendment can add it before national phase.
  2. Pure software agent metering with no bay and no power control is outside claim 1.
  3. Spec [0021] says power data alone does not prove which algorithm ran. MonoQube proves which device was powered, when, how much, and under what authorization. It does not prove what code ran.
  4. Scope can change in the international search report, written opinion, and national phase examination.

4. Recommended IP actions

  1. Confirm the assignment from inventor to Monotropic Studios LLC is recorded.
  2. File a continuation-style claim set at national phase covering the signed hash-chained record, external witness, and the two-element gate (spec support at [0039], [0077]).
  3. Decide national-phase countries by February 2028 (US, EP, JP, KR, CN, TW, IN suggested by where racks and PDUs are made).
  4. Register "MonoQube" and "MonoQube Verified" as trademarks for the conformance program.
  5. Keep the protocol spec copyrighted and the conformance suite as licensed know-how; this gives licensees a reason to pay even before grant.
